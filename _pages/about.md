I am a Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) researcher and interaction designer specializing in virtual reality (VR), spatial interaction, tangible interfaces, and emerging technologies.

My work focuses on understanding how people interact with complex digital and physical systems and translating those insights into intuitive, effective user experiences. I combine user research, experimental design, rapid prototyping, and technical development to investigate new forms of interaction and evaluate how they impact user behavior, performance, and experience.

I am currently exploring opportunities in:

* UX Research
* Design Research
* Human Factors
* XR / VR / AR Interaction Design
* Research Scientist
* Emerging Technology and Innovation roles

### About

I completed my PhD in Human-Computer Interaction at the University of Waterloo under the supervision of Dr. Mark Hancock. As a member of Touchlab and the Games Institute, my research investigated how physical and virtual worlds can be combined to support more effective interaction techniques, particularly through tangible world-in-miniature systems and immersive environments.

Prior to Waterloo, I received my M.Sc. in Computer Science from the University of Manitoba under the supervision of Dr. Pourang Irani.

### Research

My research spans Human-Computer Interaction (HCI), Virtual Reality (VR), Tangible User Interfaces (TUIs), and Haptics.

A major focus of my work is designing and evaluating world-in-miniature interaction techniques that allow users to manipulate and navigate large virtual environments through small-scale physical representations. More broadly, I am interested in how spatial interfaces, physical artifacts, and immersive technologies can improve the way people understand and interact with digital systems.

My research has contributed to the design of VR experiences, wearable interfaces, mobile interaction techniques, haptic systems, and tangible interaction paradigms, with publications appearing at leading international HCI conferences.
