## Project Details

**Venue:** Graphics Interface (GI) 2025  
**Type:** Peer-reviewed Conference Paper
**Role:** Researcher, Study Designer, Data Analyst

## Overview
![Room Design Study](/images/work/room-design-small-scale/teaser.png)

How do non-experts design a room when working at a smaller scale?

I investigated how people use three different room-design tools: a digital room planner, pen-and-paper sketches, and LEGO-based physical models. The goal was to understand how each medium influences creativity, decision making, and perceptions of scale.

## My Role

- Researcher
- Study Designer
- Data Analyst
- Co-author

## Methods

Participants designed rooms using all three approaches:

- Digital room planner
- Pen and paper
- LEGO-based miniature models

Data was collected through observation, interviews, and participant reflections.

## Key Findings

- Digital tools supported precision and refinement.
- Paper enabled quick exploration of ideas.
- LEGO models were perceived as engaging, familiar, and intuitive.
- Participants frequently struggled to estimate real-world scale when working with miniature representations.
- Different tools were preferred at different stages of the design process.

## Outcome

The project resulted in a publication at Graphics Interface 2025 and highlighted opportunities for future hybrid design tools that combine the flexibility of physical materials with the precision of digital systems.

## Skills Demonstrated

User Research · Design Research · Interviews · Observation · Qualitative Analysis · Tangible Interaction · Experimental Design

## Reflection

One of the most interesting findings was that participants naturally wanted different tools at different stages of the design process. Rather than replacing physical or paper approaches, digital tools may work best when combined with them in hybrid workflows.
